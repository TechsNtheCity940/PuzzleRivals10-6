# Spatial Spin
**Internal ID:** `spatial_spin`  
**Mode Support:** `Both`

---

# 1. Puzzle Identity

## Core Fantasy
Spatial Spin is Puzzle Rivals’ **rotation-and-orientation puzzle**. Players are not clearing clusters, not placing queued shards, and not routing systems through a network. They are rotating complex puzzle objects, sub-grids, symbols, or orientation-sensitive structures so that patterns, alignments, and directional conditions resolve correctly.

This mode should feel like:
- twisting a futuristic lock mechanism into alignment
- rotating spatial fragments until hidden order appears
- seeing the board from multiple rotational possibilities at once
- fixing orientation mismatches under pressure
- turning chaos into symmetry through precise rotation

The emotional center is:
- “this almost fits if I rotate the right section”
- “the whole problem is orientation, not location”
- “that piece is correct, but facing wrong”
- “I need to rotate this cluster without breaking the others”
- “there’s a clean spatial solution here if I stop thinking flat”

Spatial Spin must not feel like:
- Tile Shift with a rotate button
- Shard Stack without falling pieces
- Mirror Maze without light
- a basic 3x3 toy spinner solved in seconds

It needs real spatial depth.

## Cognitive Skill Tested
Primary skill:
- **rotational spatial reasoning**

Secondary skills:
- pattern recognition under transformation
- orientation tracking
- local versus global board thinking
- sequence planning
- symmetry detection
- mental rotation

## Why It Exists in Puzzle Rivals
Spatial Spin gives the roster a clean **mental rotation puzzle lane**, which is very different from:
- Shard Stack’s placement economy
- Tile Shift’s row/column manipulation
- Link Lock’s route reservation
- Circuit Clash’s logic routing
- Score Attack’s opportunistic matching

It exists to test a different type of intelligence:
- not “what can I clear”
- not “what can I connect”
- but “how does this board transform when I rotate it?”

That difference matters.

## What Makes It Unique
Spatial Spin must be clearly distinct from nearby modes:
- unlike **Tile Shift**, players rotate structures instead of shifting rows/columns
- unlike **Shard Stack**, there is no piece queue placement economy
- unlike **Mirror Maze**, rotation is not about reflecting a beam
- unlike **Circuit Clash**, orientation matters more than logic dependencies
- unlike **Maze Rush**, there is no traversal through a path maze
- unlike **RuleBreaker**, the challenge comes from spatial structure, not changing rule conditions

Spatial Spin is about **orientation, symmetry, and rotational correction**.

# 3. Core Objective

## Win Condition
A player wins by:
- completing more spatial objectives than the opponent
- or fully resolving the board first
- or having the higher rotation-solve score at timer end

## Lose Condition
A player loses by:
- resolving fewer orientation objectives
- wasting time on low-value rotations
- repeatedly disturbing nearly-correct structures
- failing to recognize the right rotational scope

## Primary Goal
Rotate board elements, clusters, or sub-grids to create valid alignments, complete patterns, or satisfy orientation conditions.

## Secondary Goals
Advanced players optimize for:
- low-rotation solutions
- multi-objective alignments from one move
- preserving solved sections while rotating unsolved ones
- recognizing global symmetry opportunities
- minimizing collateral disruption

# 4. Board Layout

## Board Shape
- square grid with orientation-sensitive elements

## Board Size
Recommended default:
- **7 x 7**

Alternative sizes:
- 6 x 6 for easier onboarding
- 8 x 8 for advanced event/ranked variants

Recommended shipping size:
- **7 x 7**

## Cell / Tile Count
- 49 active cells

## Core Board Concept
The board is composed of orientation-sensitive pieces that may include:
- directional glyph tiles
- arrow-like crystal fragments
- quarter-circle or arc fragments
- half-shapes that only form valid patterns when rotated correctly
- symbol clusters
- mini-subgrid rotation hubs
- anchor tiles that do not rotate
- locked tiles in advanced variants

## Required Structure Rule
A standard Spatial Spin board must include:
- at least **3 major rotational decision layers**
- at least **1 local rotation that helps short term but harms global alignment**
- at least **1 puzzle area where rotating a sub-grid is better than rotating individual cells**
- at least **1 symmetry or pattern goal requiring multi-step setup**
- at least **1 orientation trap where shape placement looks correct but directional facing is wrong**
- at least **1 opportunity for one smart rotation to resolve multiple objectives**

This keeps the mode from becoming a one-note spin-until-it-fits toy.

## Board Objective Types
Spatial Spin boards may ask players to:
- align directional symbols into correct facing
- complete a rotationally valid shape
- build symmetry across an axis
- restore broken circular or geometric motifs
- orient all arrows toward a core
- orient shards so energy flow direction is valid
- rotate subgrids to complete a master pattern

## Reserved HUD Space
Must preserve room for:
- top center: timer + current objective
- side or top-adjacent: progress / score / solve count
- bottom left/right: player cards and usernames
- optional small orientation preview or target diagram strip

## Mobile Readability Notes
- rotated state must be instantly visible
- symbols must read cleanly at small size
- orientation-sensitive art must not be ambiguous
- sub-grid selection / rotation highlight must be obvious
- solved patterns must stand out clearly from almost-solved patterns

# 5. Controls and Inputs

## Primary Input
Recommended default:
- tap a tile or sub-grid selection zone, then rotate clockwise

## Secondary Input
- optional long-press for counterclockwise
- optional rotate-left / rotate-right buttons in accessibility mode
- optional tap-hold to preview resulting orientation

## Input Rules
Players may rotate:
- individual rotatable tiles
- designated sub-grid clusters
- in some variants, whole rings or grouped rotational zones

Recommended base mode:
- **mix of individual tile rotations and designated sub-grid rotations**

This is what makes the mode more interesting than a simple one-piece spinner.

## Invalid Input Behavior
If invalid:
- locked tile rejects
- anchored zone refuses to rotate
- brief feedback only
- no large punishment beyond time loss

# 6. Solving Rules

## How the Puzzle Is Solved
Players solve by rotating pieces or sub-structures until the board satisfies orientation objectives.

Recommended base rule set:
- one or more target patterns are shown
- current board contains the correct pieces but many are facing incorrectly
- player must rotate local or grouped sections to satisfy the target condition

## Solve Sequence
1. inspect current objective
2. identify the incorrectly oriented section
3. decide whether to rotate individual tile or larger rotational cluster
4. perform rotation
5. evaluate resulting local and global board impact
6. continue until objective is satisfied
7. next stage or new objective activates

## Partial Progress Rules
- solved local structures may remain solved between stages if the mode supports persistent board progression
- rotating a larger cluster may disturb previously solved fragments
- partial correctness matters visually but does not award full completion unless objective conditions are met

## Failure Cases
- rotating the wrong scope
- fixing one tile while breaking the broader pattern
- not noticing that direction, not position, is the real problem
- solving local fragments that never contribute to the objective
- over-rotating and losing efficient progress

## Skill Ceiling Behavior
Top players separate themselves by:
- seeing rotational consequences before acting
- recognizing whether local or global rotation is needed
- exploiting one rotation to fix multiple elements
- preserving solved sections
- spotting symmetry and directional coherence faster than the opponent

# 7. Scoring Model

## Base Points
Recommended:
- minor orientation objective resolved: **25 points**
- medium pattern completion: **40 points**
- symmetry restoration: **50 points**
- major full-board spatial objective: **75 points**

## Bonus Points
- low-rotation solution: **+10**
- one rotation fixing multiple target elements: **+12**
- no-break bonus for preserving already-solved structures: **+8**
- perfect symmetry solve: **+15**
- sub-grid master rotation solving two sectors at once: **+18**

## Penalties
- no direct score loss in base mode
- optional ranked penalty:
  - repeated useless rotations reduce efficiency bonus eligibility
- wrong rotations are punished mainly through tempo loss and board disruption

## Score Target to Win
Recommended BvB target:
- **260 to 320**

Recommended default:
- **300**

## Tie Rules
1. more completed objectives wins
2. if tied, fewer rotations used wins
3. if tied, faster final objective completion wins

## Recommended Score Tuning
Expected 90-second outcomes:
- average player: partial objective resolution and one major completion
- good player: repeated medium/major objective clears
- strong player: efficient low-rotation, multi-objective resolutions

# 8. Pressure Systems

## Main Pressure Source
- rotational efficiency pressure

## Secondary Pressure Source
- timer pressure

The player should feel:
- “I can fix this with one smart turn or ruin it with three bad ones”
- “this board wants a global solution, not brute force”
- “I’m losing time rotating pieces that are already almost right”
- “the opponent is seeing the orientation faster than I am”

## Escalation Model
Pressure rises through:
- more overlapping solved/unsolved regions
- more sub-grid rotation complexity
- more symmetry-dependent targets
- less forgiving endgame time windows

## Comeback Potential
Comeback is moderate:
- one very efficient multi-fix rotation can swing tempo
- opponents can waste huge time on inefficient spins
- major objectives provide meaningful score spikes

This mode should reward insight, not random motion.

# 10. Puzzle Flow

- countdown
- objective shown clearly
- board starts partially scrambled
- first meaningful solution path visible, but not trivial

- inspect pattern
- rotate local or grouped section
- evaluate
- resolve objective

## Endgame Flow
Final 20 seconds:
- safer efficient corrections become more valuable than greedy perfect-board plans
- final objective decisions get tighter
- players may abandon elegance for practical completion

## Overtime / Sudden Death
Only for exact tie states:
- next objective completion wins

- objective complexity
- directional ambiguity
- symmetry dependence

# 20. Final Design Summary

## One-Paragraph Summary
Spatial Spin is Puzzle Rivals’ rotation-and-orientation puzzle mode. Players manipulate individual tiles and designated sub-grid clusters on a 7x7 board to resolve directional, geometric, and symmetry-based objectives. The puzzle emphasizes mental rotation, structural foresight, and the ability to recognize whether a local or global rotational move will create the cleanest solution. It should feel elegant, intelligent, and distinct from both Tile Shift’s translation-based manipulation and Shard Stack’s queue-based placement gameplay.

## Production Priority
- **High**

- glyph atlas
- tile/sub-grid selection and rotation FX
- symmetry and objective completion FX

# Mirror Maze
**Internal ID:** `mirror_maze`  
**Mode Support:** `Both`

---

# 1. Puzzle Identity

## Core Fantasy
Mirror Maze is Puzzle Rivals’ **light-routing illusion puzzle**. Players do not simply navigate a maze or connect nodes. They manipulate mirrors, prisms, shutters, blockers, and hidden corridors so beams of light can travel through the board, reveal unseen spaces, unlock obstructions, split into multiple paths, and ultimately satisfy layered objective conditions.

This mode must feel:
- mysterious
- elegant
- high-stakes
- visually magical but mechanically precise
- like solving a light machine hidden inside a maze

It must **not** feel like:
- a 3-second “bounce beam to target” toy
- Maze Rush with shinier art
- Circuit Clash with mirrors
- a children’s reflection puzzle with one obvious answer

The emotional center is:
- “I know there’s more of the maze hidden beyond this corridor”
- “if I angle this beam into that prism, I can light both branches”
- “I need to reveal that dark chamber before I commit the main beam”
- “that blocker has to be removed indirectly”
- “this puzzle is about sequence, not just a single reflection”

## Cognitive Skill Tested
Primary skill:
- **multi-step reflective path planning**

Secondary skills:
- angle prediction
- sequence logic
- reveal management
- branch ordering
- hidden-space deduction
- beam splitting logic
- layered objective planning

## Why It Exists in Puzzle Rivals
Mirror Maze gives the roster a highly creative **optics-and-discovery puzzle**. It fills a distinct niche:
- more visually dramatic than Link Lock
- more mysterious than Circuit Clash
- more sequence-driven than Maze Rush
- more layered and theatrical than Pipe Pulse

This mode should be one of the roster’s standout “wow, that’s a different kind of puzzle” experiences.

## What Makes It Unique
Mirror Maze must be clearly different from nearby modes:
- unlike **Maze Rush**, the player is not navigating themselves through the maze. They are routing light through it.
- unlike **Circuit Clash**, the puzzle is not primarily electrical logic. It is optical routing, reveal sequencing, and reflection structure.
- unlike **Link Lock**, the board is not solved by static endpoint linking.
- unlike **Pipe Pulse**, there is no real-time pressure flow instability as the main identity.
- unlike **RuleBreaker**, complexity comes from structure, not changing rule sets.

Mirror Maze is about:
- reflection
- reveal
- hidden information
- optical sequence logic
- multi-stage beam routing

# 3. Core Objective

## Win Condition
A player wins by:
- completing more mirror-maze objective stages than the opponent
- or fully solving the board first
- or having higher stage score when time expires

## Lose Condition
A player loses by:
- falling behind in stage completion
- mis-sequencing reveal steps
- wasting too much time on dead beams
- failing to unlock blocked mirrors or shutters efficiently

## Primary Goal
Route light through the maze to:
1. reveal hidden spaces
2. activate unlock targets
3. split beams where needed
4. hit required mirror and objective nodes
5. ultimately satisfy the final exit or completion target

## Secondary Goals
Advanced players optimize for:
- low-rotation or low-move solutions
- early reveal of critical hidden corridors
- efficient prism use
- beam reuse across multiple objectives
- clean sequencing that avoids unnecessary beam resets

## Required Structure Rule
A standard Mirror Maze board must include:
- at least **1 primary source emitter**
- at least **3 major route decision layers**
- at least **2 false or inefficient beam paths**
- at least **1 hidden corridor or dark chamber**
- at least **1 prism that meaningfully splits a beam**
- at least **1 blocked mirror or shuttered segment**
- at least **1 unlock target that must be hit indirectly**
- at least **3 objective stages** before final completion

That means the player is not solving one simple reflection. They are solving a layered optical machine.

- light source emitters

- rotatable mirrors
- prisms that split beams
- refractor nodes
- shutters
- blocked mirrors

- reveal-only chambers

- blocked mirrors and shutters must be unmistakable
- all effects must support path readability, not obscure it

# 5. Controls and Inputs

## Primary Input
Recommended default:
- tap to rotate selected mirror or prism orientation

## Secondary Input
- optional long-press to rotate backward
- tap source to re-fire beam after changes if the board uses discrete beam pulses
- tap-hold for preview ray in accessibility mode only

## Input Rules
Players may:
- rotate rotatable mirrors
- rotate some prisms if allowed by board rules
- trigger beam pulse from source
- use reflected beam to activate other board features

Some elements are fixed:
- fixed mirrors
- certain prisms
- structural walls
- some shutters before unlocked

## Invalid Input Behavior
If invalid:
- blocked tile rejects
- fixed mirror does not rotate
- shuttered piece remains inactive until unlocked
- brief visual reject only

Wrong actions should waste time, not create visual chaos.

# 6. Solving Rules

## How the Puzzle Is Solved
Players solve Mirror Maze by creating correct light routes through mirrors and prisms so that:
- hidden corridors are revealed
- blocked paths are unlocked
- multi-stage beam sequences are completed
- final target receptors are illuminated in the correct structure

## Required Solve Structure
A standard solve should usually include the following type of sequence:
1. fire or route beam into first mirror lane
2. reveal a hidden corridor or chamber
3. use newly visible information to angle beam toward an unlock target
4. remove or open a blocker / shutter
5. route beam into a prism to split toward multiple areas
6. activate secondary mirrors or receptors
7. complete final endpoint objective

That is the minimum kind of layered solve logic that keeps the puzzle interesting.

## Solve Sequence
1. identify visible route start
2. rotate or align early mirrors
3. send beam to reveal hidden section
4. inspect newly visible corridor
5. reroute beam toward unlock target
6. clear blocker or open shutter
7. use prism to split beam if multiple objectives need simultaneous activation
8. satisfy final receptor condition

## Partial Progress Rules
- hidden area reveals persist once activated unless the board explicitly uses reset states
- unlocked shutters remain open unless specified otherwise
- some objectives may require simultaneous beam states
- some board progress is stateful and accumulates across steps

## Failure Cases
- routing beam down pretty but useless corridor
- unlocking the wrong chamber first
- using prism too early and losing beam efficiency
- failing to reveal enough of the maze before pursuing final route
- opening one line but blocking another through bad mirror orientation
- ignoring sequence order and trying to brute-force final target

## Skill Ceiling Behavior
Top players separate themselves by:
- predicting multiple reflections ahead
- recognizing where hidden chambers likely matter
- using prisms only when strategically correct
- sequencing reveal and unlock actions in the best order
- minimizing wasted beam resets

# 7. Scoring Model

## Base Points
Recommended:
- hidden corridor revealed: **20 points**
- blocker/shutter unlocked: **30 points**
- objective crystal activated: **40 points**
- dual-branch prism success: **45 points**
- final receptor / exit completion: **80 points**

## Bonus Points
- low-rotation solve bonus: **+10**
- no wasted beam reset in stage: **+12**
- hidden chamber first-try reveal optimization: **+10**
- correct prism split that solves two objectives in one sequence: **+18**
- perfect full-board optical solve: **+30**

## Penalties
- wrong beam path: no progress, time loss only
- repeated useless resets: optional small score tax in ranked
- brute-force misrotation spam: indirect punishment through time loss

## Score Target to Win
Recommended BvB default:
- **260 to 320**

Recommended default:
- **300**

## Tie Rules
1. farther objective stage wins
2. if tied, fewer beam resets wins
3. if tied, fewer invalid rotations wins
4. if tied, faster final objective completion wins

## Recommended Score Tuning
Expected 100-second outcomes:
- average player: partial multi-stage completion
- good player: one full board solve plus strong second-board progress
- strong player: repeated clean multi-stage solves with efficiency bonuses

# 8. Pressure Systems

## Main Pressure Source
- sequence complexity pressure

## Secondary Pressure Source
- hidden-information pressure

Unlike Combo Rush, pressure is not raw speed panic. It comes from:
- not knowing the full board at first
- needing to reveal before committing
- sequencing light logic correctly
- choosing whether to pursue unlocks, reveals, or split routes first

## Escalation Model
Pressure rises through:
- more hidden chambers
- more blocked mirror states
- more expensive wrong sequencing
- later-stage multi-branch beam requirements
- tighter endgame timer

## Comeback Potential
Comeback is moderate:
- players can recover by finding the correct reveal sequence quickly
- opponents can lose major time on bad unlock order
- strong prism usage can compress multiple objectives and swing tempo

- blocker dependencies

- number of hidden cells/chambers

- prism count

# 17. Anti-Overlap Check

## Closest Puzzle Comparisons
- Maze Rush
- Circuit Clash
- Link Lock

## How It Is Different
Compared to Maze Rush:
- players route light, not themselves
- hidden corridors are revealed by beam interaction
- optics sequencing matters more than navigation memory

Compared to Circuit Clash:
- optics and reflection are the identity, not electrical logic and branching circuits

Compared to Link Lock:
- state changes, reveals, and split beams make it much more dynamic than static pair-connection planning

## What Must Never Happen
- must not become a one-bounce toy puzzle
- must not be solvable in seconds
- must not reduce prisms to decorative extras
- must not hide important beam readability behind overdone bloom
- must not feel like Maze Rush reskinned with mirrors

# 20. Final Design Summary

## One-Paragraph Summary
Mirror Maze is Puzzle Rivals’ optics-and-discovery puzzle mode. Players manipulate mirrors, prisms, blockers, and hidden corridors on a 9x9 board to route light through a layered reflective maze. Solves require revealing unseen spaces, unlocking blocked mirrors, splitting beams to satisfy multiple targets, and sequencing these actions correctly before reaching the final receptor. The mode must feel clever, luminous, and significantly more complex than a simple bounce-the-beam puzzle, with strong emphasis on multi-stage reflective logic rather than quick one-step solutions.

## Production Priority
- **High**

## Dependencies
- optics atlas
- beam travel / reflect / prism split FX
- hidden reveal and blocker unlock FX
- stage progress lane assets
- player card shimmer
- fairness-seeded reflective board generation
